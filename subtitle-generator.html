The direct answer

To generate subtitles, open the Instagram Transcript tool, paste a public Instagram Reel or video URL, or select a saved MP4, MOV, WebM, MP3, WAV, M4A, or OGG file. InstaScript recognizes English speech in your browser and returns timestamped cues you can download as SRT or TXT.

  1. Use a public URL that works without a login, or choose a file you may process.
  2. Let the browser create speech segments and timestamps.
  3. Replay important cues, correct wording and timing, then export SRT for a subtitle player or TXT for notes.

Boundary: this is a speech subtitle draft. It does not burn captions into video, read text in frames, translate speech, add visual styling, or identify speakers.

Public URL or local file

A public Instagram URL can fail when the media is private, removed, region-limited, expired, or protected by a login. Save a copy you are allowed to use and upload it instead. Uploads are limited to 100 MB and decoded on your device. URL fetch time is separate from local transcription.

Review before publishing

  • Replay words covered by music, effects, echo, clipping, or crosstalk.
  • Check accents, names, numbers, product terms, and URLs against the recording.
  • Split long cues and remove overlaps when viewers need more reading time.
  • Text printed in frames needs visual inspection or OCR; speech recognition cannot see it.
  • The current browser model is English-focused, so non-English speech needs full manual review.

FAQ

Does this burn subtitles into my video?

No. It creates editable TXT and SRT files. A video editor can style or burn them later.

Does it copy the post's written caption?

No. It transcribes spoken audio. The written post description is a separate piece of content.

Is an uploaded recording stored?

Uploaded media is decoded and transcribed in your browser. A public URL needs a temporary media fetch before local processing; the transcript is not saved as an account record.

Is InstaScript affiliated with Instagram?

No. InstaScript is independent of Instagram and Meta.
