The direct answer

To convert a WhatsApp voice message to text, save or export the audio file you are allowed to use, then open the InstaScript tool and choose it. The browser recognizes supported English speech and gives you timestamped text with TXT, SRT and VTT downloads.

  1. Export the voice message to your device with the sender's permission.
  2. Select the local audio file in InstaScript and let the browser process it.
  3. Replay important segments, correct names and numbers, then copy the transcript or download TXT/SRT/VTT.

Link boundary: a private WhatsApp chat or message URL is not fetched. InstaScript works from a permitted local file, and it transcribes speech rather than message text, stickers, images or speaker identity.

WhatsApp file or private link?

Use an audio file that you may process. A chat link, cloud backup, disappearing message, account-only media, or a file blocked by device permissions cannot be read by this page. If the saved file is not a browser-readable audio format, export it again or use a permitted compatible copy. Uploads are limited to 100 MB.

Review a voice message transcript

  • Replay words covered by music, background noise, clipping, echo or overlapping speakers.
  • Check names, phone numbers, prices, dates and links against the recording.
  • Expect weaker results for very short words, whispers, accents and non-English speech; the current model is English-focused.
  • Text visible in an image or video is not part of speech transcription.
  • Remove private details from any copy you share beyond the people who authorized the recording.

FAQ

Can I paste a WhatsApp message link?

No. Use a permitted local audio file exported from the message.

Does it transcribe the chat text?

No. It recognizes spoken audio only. The written message, reactions and attachments are separate content.

Is the voice message uploaded?

Local files are decoded and transcribed in your browser. The transcript is not saved as an account record.

Can I create subtitles from the result?

Yes. Download SRT, review its cues, and edit it in the Subtitle Editor if needed.

Export your edited transcript

In the shared transcription tool, Copy and TXT use your edited words. They are plain text by default; enable Include timestamps in TXT and Copy to add available start times. This option does not run speech recognition again. SRT, VTT and Open in Subtitle Editor require non-empty cue text and complete valid start/end times. Missing end times are not estimated. If timing is incomplete, keep the recording for review and copy or download TXT instead.
