Phone Call Transcription: Capture, Find, and Check the Conversation

Instagram Transcript Video to Text YouTube Transcript About Contact

Phone call transcription turns captured speech into a readable record that you can search and revisit. For an iPhone call you need to keep, use a supported call-recording feature, then open the saved recording in Notes to read the transcript and replay the relevant passage. Keep the recording and transcript together: the text helps you locate a moment, while playback lets you check what was actually said.

Record the conversation you want to retain

On a supported iPhone, the current documented route is Phone → More → Call Recording during a call. Make sure the other participant is willing to be recorded; the feature also plays an audible recording notice. Tap Stop to finish recording before the call ends, or end the call to stop it. Open the saved call in Notes, select its recording, and use the transcript to search or jump to a spoken passage. Apple Support

Resolve the version mismatch before changing tools

“iOS 18” is an incomplete compatibility check: the documented starting point for recording calls and viewing their words in Notes is iOS 18.1. Check Settings → General → About for the installed version rather than relying on the major version alone. An older interface may show a recording icon near the top of the call screen, while the current instructions put Call Recording under More. A different-looking control is a reason to check the instructions for your software version, not proof that transcription has disappeared. Transcription and an AI summary are separate capabilities. An iPhone 15 Pro is an Apple Intelligence-compatible model, but a summary still depends on supported software, language, region, and Apple Intelligence being enabled. Missing a summary therefore does not, by itself, prove that the recorded call lacks a transcript. Apple Support Device requirements

The recording boundary is also the transcript boundary

Starting a recording during a call gives you the recorded portion, not a retrospective copy of everything already said. The same boundary matters at the end: you can stop recording while continuing the conversation. Our practical interpretation is that a transcript should travel with a clear statement of which part of the call was captured. Keep any notes typed during the conversation distinct from the words produced by transcription. A useful distinction is “what I noted” versus “what the recording contains,” especially when the note and the call recording live in the same app.

A searchable name can still be the wrong name

Speaker labels, search, and synchronized playback make a transcript easier to navigate; they do not establish that every recognized word is correct. A proper name can be replaced by a plausible-sounding word even when the surrounding sentence reads smoothly. Use the text as a route back to the disputed passage, then listen before quoting it. Our editorial rule is to treat a transcript used for coaching as a locator, not as a verdict on the speaker. Reading a call quickly can surface a training question, but the recording supplies the context needed to discuss it.

Screening a caller answers a different question

Call Screening is designed to help you decide whether to answer an unknown caller. In the iOS 26 workflow, Settings → Apps → Phone → Screen Unknown Callers → Ask Reason for Calling asks the caller for identifying information before the phone rings. You can read a live transcript and decide whether to join the conversation. That is different from deliberately recording a conversation and returning to its saved transcript afterward. Our interpretation: seeing words during screening is not evidence that the conversation after pickup has been saved as a complete call record. Choose the feature by the record you need at the end: a reason to answer, a captured passage to revisit, or a transcript you can search alongside its audio. Apple Support
