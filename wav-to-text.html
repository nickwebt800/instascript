WAV to text: the direct answer

WAV to text means turning the spoken audio in a WAV recording into searchable words. Choose one local file above, press Transcribe this file, and review timestamped segments. Copy the transcript, download .txt for notes, or download .srt for timed subtitle cues.

WAV often stores uncompressed PCM, but the browser still has to decode the actual header and sample format. This page reads a local recording; it does not upload the audio, inspect a cloud link, or turn the filename into text.

How to transcribe a WAV recording

  1. Choose one WAV. Use a file up to 100 MB and keep the original beside the result.
  2. Start the browser model. The first run downloads the speech model into cache; later runs can reuse it on the same device.
  3. Review and export. Replay names, numbers and dates at their timestamps, then copy TXT or download SRT.

Example WAV transcript

[00:00] The interview starts after the tone
[00:08] Please read the reference number back
[00:16] We will send the notes this afternoon

TXT is easy to search. SRT preserves numbered cues and time ranges. Neither export adds speaker labels or caption styling.

Privacy and WAV limits

The selected WAV is decoded and transcribed in your browser. There is no account, transcript history or server upload for this local-file workflow. A damaged header, unusual sample encoding, clipping, music, echo or overlapping voices can cause an error or missing words. It does not translate or identify speakers.

How to check the result

  • Replay every timestamp where a name, number or date matters.
  • Check quiet passages, room noise and crosstalk around short words.
  • Compare the opening and closing seconds after an abrupt recording cut.
  • Keep the original WAV beside the TXT or SRT so edits remain traceable.

FAQ

Can I paste a WAV link?

No. Save a recording you are allowed to use and choose the local file. Private cloud links are not fetched.

Does the WAV leave my device?

No for this local workflow. The model runs in the browser and the page does not keep a server copy.

What if the WAV will not decode?

Open it in a player first or export a standard PCM WAV or MP3. A .wav suffix alone does not guarantee a browser-readable sample format.

Can I make subtitles from the result?

Yes. Download SRT and inspect cue breaks in a subtitle editor. The export contains speech timing, not styling or a video preview.

Why is the first run slower?

The first run loads and initializes the speech model. A later file can reuse the cached model.
