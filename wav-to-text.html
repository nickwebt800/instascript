WAV to text: the direct answer

WAV to text means turning the spoken audio in a WAV recording into searchable words. Choose one local file above, press Transcribe this file, and review timestamped segments. Copy the transcript, download .txt for notes, or download .srt for timed subtitle cues.

WAV often stores uncompressed PCM, but the browser still has to decode the actual header and sample format. This page reads a local recording; it does not upload the audio, inspect a cloud link, or turn the filename into text.

How to transcribe a WAV recording

  1. Choose one WAV. Use a file up to 100 MB and keep the original beside the result.
  2. Start the browser model. The first run downloads the speech model into cache; later runs can reuse it on the same device.
  3. Review and export. Replay names, numbers and dates at their timestamps, then copy TXT or download SRT.

Example WAV transcript

[00:00] The interview starts after the tone
[00:08] Please read the reference number back
[00:16] We will send the notes this afternoon

TXT is easy to search. SRT preserves numbered cues and time ranges. Neither export adds speaker labels or caption styling.

Privacy and WAV limits

The selected WAV is decoded and transcribed in your browser. There is no account, transcript history or server upload for this local-file workflow. A damaged header, unusual sample encoding, clipping, music, echo or overlapping voices can cause an error or missing words. It does not translate or identify speakers.

How to check the result

  • Replay every timestamp where a name, number or date matters.
  • Check quiet passages, room noise and crosstalk around short words.
  • Compare the opening and closing seconds after an abrupt recording cut.
  • Keep the original WAV beside the TXT, SRT or VTT so edits remain traceable.

FAQ

Can I paste a WAV link?

No. Save a recording you are allowed to use and choose the local file. Private cloud links are not fetched.

Does the WAV leave my device?

No for this local workflow. The model runs in the browser and the page does not keep a server copy.

What if the WAV will not decode?

Open it in a player first or export a standard PCM WAV or MP3. A .wav suffix alone does not guarantee a browser-readable sample format.

Can I make subtitles from the result?

Yes. Download SRT and inspect cue breaks in a subtitle editor. The export contains speech timing, not styling or a video preview.

Why is the first run slower?

The first run loads and initializes the speech model. A later file can reuse the cached model.

Cancel and retry

Only one transcription task can run at a time. Cancel processing discards its result; a model download or local audio/model operation already in progress may need to finish first. After a failure or cancellation, Try the same input again reuses the selected file in this tab. Selecting a different input clears that retry target. Local file contents are not uploaded. Model loading and speech recognition do not have a fixed timeout.

Review and edit your transcript

After transcription, replay the original recording with the player and click a timestamp to seek to a cue. Edit the words in each cue; Copy, TXT, SRT and VTT use your edits. When every cue has complete valid timing, choose Open in Subtitle Editor to change timing in another tab. Recording playback depends on your browser codec support. Subtitle Editor receives your transcript and recording locally between same-origin tabs; no project is saved.

Export your edited transcript

In the shared transcription tool, Copy and TXT use your edited words. They are plain text by default; enable Include timestamps in TXT, Markdown and Copy to add available start times. This option does not run speech recognition again. SRT, VTT and Open in Subtitle Editor require non-empty cue text and complete valid start/end times. Missing end times are not estimated. If timing is incomplete, keep the recording for review and copy or download TXT instead.

Search your current transcript

After transcription, use Find in this transcript to search your latest edited words. Previous and Next move between occurrences; the preview highlights one match. Choose Edit this match to select its text, or play its cue when a recording and start time are available. Enter moves forward, Shift+Enter moves back, and Escape in the search box clears the query.

Search is literal, ignores ASCII letter case, and stays inside each cue in your current tab. It does not upload or save your query, change subtitle timing, or alter exports.

Export Markdown notes

Choose Markdown to save a .md note with a title and your latest edited words, one paragraph per speech cue (or the available untimed text). Include timestamps in TXT, Markdown and Copy adds only available start times; missing times are not estimated. Markdown punctuation and HTML-like words are escaped as text. The note is built in your browser without uploading content or adding it to a history. This exports a transcript, not an AI summary.
