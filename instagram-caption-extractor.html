The short answer

An Instagram caption extractor can mean two different things. The post caption is the written text below a post; the video transcript is the speech in the audio. InstaScript handles the second: open the Instagram Transcript tool, paste a public URL or upload a saved video, then review and download the spoken words as TXT or SRT.

  1. Decide whether you need the post description, words spoken in the video, or text visible in its frames.
  2. For spoken words, paste a public URL or upload an MP4, MOV or WebM file.
  3. Check the timestamped result against the audio before quoting or publishing it.

Boundary: the transcript tool does not scrape Instagram's written caption, comments, hashtags, or text printed inside a frame.

Calling all three “captions” creates bad exports. A post description may contain hashtags that are never spoken, while a transcript may contain words absent from the description. Keep the source type in the filename or notes so a later editor knows what was extracted.

How to extract and verify spoken caption text

Use a public URL only when the post opens without a login and its media can be fetched. A private, removed, region-limited, or expired link can return a page without playable audio. Save a copy you have permission to process and upload that file instead. Uploads accept MP4, MOV and WebM files up to 100 MB.

After processing, compare the transcript with the waveform or replay each important segment. A useful review pass checks names, numbers, URLs, product terms, and any sentence spoken over music. Mark uncertain words rather than silently “fixing” them.

[00:00] The caption says one thing
[00:04] but the speaker adds a second detail
[00:09] so I keep both sources in my notes

Download TXT for searchable notes and SRT when an editor or player needs numbered subtitle cues. The SRT contains recognized speech segments and timestamps; it is not a copy of Instagram's post caption.

Accuracy checks that catch real errors

  • Music: replay short sections where lyrics or sound effects overlap speech.
  • Two speakers: label the speaker yourself; the output is text with timing, not speaker diarization.
  • Accents and names: check proper nouns against the original audio and a trusted spelling.
  • Non-English speech: treat the English-focused browser model as a draft and verify every important line.
  • On-screen text: pause the video and inspect the frame; speech recognition cannot see pixels.

Privacy and processing boundary

For an uploaded file, decoding and speech recognition run on your device. A public URL still needs a network fetch to obtain media before local processing begins. No account record is created for the transcript, but you remain responsible for permission to save and use the video.

FAQ

Can this copy the written Instagram post caption?

No. This page points you to the distinction and transcribes spoken audio. Copy the written description from the post itself when you are allowed to access it.

Can it read words displayed in a Reel?

No. Text burned into frames requires visual inspection or OCR. The transcript covers speech only.

What should I publish after extraction?

Keep the original media, the TXT or SRT file, and a short review note with the timestamps you checked. That makes corrections traceable.

Is InstaScript affiliated with Instagram?

No. InstaScript is an independent project and is not affiliated with Instagram or Meta.

Respect privacy, permission, and copyright when saving, extracting, or republishing Instagram content.
