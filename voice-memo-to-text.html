Voice memo to text: the direct answer

Voice memo to text means turning speech in a recording into searchable words. Choose one local memo above, press Transcribe this file, and review timestamped segments. Copy the transcript, download .txt for notes, or download .srt for timed subtitle cues.

This workflow fits an iPhone Voice Memos export or another recording you are allowed to use. The page reads the audio track from the file; it does not fetch a phone backup, cloud link, title or metadata as a transcript.

How to transcribe a voice memo

  1. Export one recording. Choose a browser-readable M4A, MP3, WAV or OGG file up to 100 MB.
  2. Start the browser model. The first run downloads the speech model into cache; later runs on the same device can reuse it.
  3. Review and export. Replay names, numbers and dates at their timestamps, then copy TXT or download SRT.

Example voice memo transcript

[00:00] Call the clinic before ten
[00:07] Ask about the revised appointment time
[00:15] Add the confirmation number to the notes

TXT is easy to search. SRT preserves numbered cues and time ranges. Neither format adds speaker labels or styling.

Privacy and voice memo limits

The selected recording is decoded and transcribed in your browser. There is no account, transcript history or server upload for this local-file workflow. Save the result before closing the tab.

The model is intended for English speech. Unsupported codecs, DRM, music, echo, clipping and overlapping voices can cause an error or missing words. It does not translate or identify speakers.

How to check the result

  • Replay every timestamp where a name, number or date matters.
  • Check music, room noise and crosstalk around short words.
  • Compare the opening and closing seconds after an abrupt recording cut.
  • Keep the original memo beside the TXT or SRT so edits remain traceable.

FAQ

Can I paste a voice memo link?

No. Save a recording you are permitted to use and choose the local file. Private cloud links are not fetched.

Does the memo leave my device?

No for this local workflow. The model runs in the browser and the page does not keep a server copy.

What if the memo will not decode?

Try opening it in a player or exporting a browser-friendly WAV or MP3. The extension alone does not identify its codec.

Can I make subtitles from the result?

Yes. Download SRT and inspect cue breaks in a subtitle editor. The export contains speech timing, not styling or a video preview.

Why is the first run slower?

The first run loads and initializes the speech model. A later file can reuse the cached model.
