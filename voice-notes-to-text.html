1. Keep an original transcript and its source

Save the recording and a first text copy before reorganizing anything. Include the recording date in your own notes so relative dates such as tomorrow remain interpretable. Use Voice Memo to Text for a local browser-decodable English recording up to 100 MB.

Check: You can still identify which words came from the recording.

2. Make corrections that you can justify by listening

In this invented example, the source says: “Send the draft on Friday, unless Jordan has not approved it.” Replay Friday, unless and not. If the audio does not establish approval, mark it unknown; a plausible interpretation is not evidence.

Check: Negations, conditions, names and dates survive the review.

3. Write a separate working note

A manual note might read: “Draft delivery: Friday, conditional on Jordan approval. Approval status: unknown; confirm first.” This is an editorial note, not the verbatim quote. Keep the quote and your decision separately labelled. InstaScript does not extract tasks or automatically summarize the recording.

Check: Another reader can distinguish the spoken statement, your correction and your proposed action.

More questions and limits

What processing and privacy boundaries apply?

Local decoding, recognition, replay, edits and downloads run in your browser. Model resources need network access, but the local recording and transcript are not uploaded. There is no account history or saved cloud project; download your work before closing the tab. Same-origin editor transfer keeps the cues and recording local between tabs. Instagram URL retrieval and YouTube caption lookup have separate server-assisted privacy boundaries.

Where should I continue?
